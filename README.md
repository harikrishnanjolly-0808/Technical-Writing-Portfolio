# Preparing a Document from Scratch - Framework

The process of developing and writing a document from scratch closely mirrors the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC). As a Technical Writer, you must ensure that every document serves a clear purpose, targets a specific audience, and fulfills a defined need. 

To streamline this process, use the **POWER** acronym framework:
* **P** – Planning
* **O** – Organizing
* **W** – Writing
* **E** – Editing
* **R** – Release

---

## 1. Planning
Planning is the initial stage where you define the goals, purpose, scope, and target audience for the document.

### Key Steps
* **Type of Document:** Identify if you are creating a User Guide, Admin Guide, API Documentation, or Release Notes.
* **Define Target Audience:** Map out reader requirements.
* **Select Assets:** Determine the tools, templates, and style guides for the document.
* **Project Management:** Establish necessary resources and timelines.
* **Outputs:** Audience analysis, documentation plan, content outline, template, and style guide.

### Audience Analysis
Understanding your readers ensures the document meets their exact expectations. Use the following criteria to profile them:

* **Demographics:** Age, location, language, and education.
* **Professional Background:** Job role, industry, and technical expertise.
* **Purpose:** Why do they need this document? (e.g., to solve a problem, configure a software or function, or learn a new skill).

### Understanding Audience Knowledge Level
Categorize your readers into one of three tiers to customize your approach:
* **Beginner:** Requires simple language, step-by-step instructions, and visuals.
* **Intermediate:** Requires concise instructions, assuming basic familiarity.
* **Advanced:** Requires technical explanations and references.

### Customized Content
Tailor your document elements directly to your target audience profiles:
* **Tone:** Maintain a formal tone for professional audiences.
* **Structure:** Add navigational aids like glossaries and FAQs.
* **Visuals:** Integrate diagrams to explain technical features.

### Time Estimation
When planning your schedule, calculate your time investments based on these key variables:
* **Unknown Factor:** Is it a new product or feature?
* **Complexity:** Is it difficult to set up or use, requiring help until the user is familiar?
* **Lengthiness:** Is the procedure long with multiple steps or features?
* **Risk:** What potential risks or dependencies are involved?

---

## 2. Organizing
Organizing involves gathering and structuring raw information to ensure your documentation remains accurate, correct, and complete.

### Key Steps
* Interact with Subject Matter Experts (SMEs), developers, and Product Managers.
* Study the product or software through a hands-on approach.
* Analyze existing documents.
* **Outputs:** Collected data, use cases, and technical details.

---

## 3. Writing
This is the initial draft preparation stage where you start working on the first version using the planned template and structure. You build content based on relevant available information and avoid all irrelevant details.

### Key Steps
* **Self-Review:** Analyze your initial draft to confirm all information is included and structured as per the planned strategy.
* **Peer Review:** Route the draft to other technical writers to catch unintended mistakes.
* **Technical Review:** Send the draft to SMEs or developers to verify technical accuracy.
* **Final Edit:** Prepare the final edit for grammar and adherence to guidelines and compliances.
* **Outputs:** A thoroughly reviewed and improved documentation draft.

---

## 4. Release
This is the final stage of releasing the document to the client.

### Maintenance
As a Technical Writer, you must keep all documentation relevant and up to date as per the changing requirements and improvements needed over time.

### Key Steps
* Update the content for new product features.
* Respond to user feedback.
* Archive obsolete versions and maintain version control.
* **Outputs:** An updated and well-maintained documentation asset.
